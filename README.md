## 🌟 About the project

**FutureBite** is a career-discovery website concept developed for the **Birmingham City University (BCU) × Mondelēz STEAM Hackathon 2026**.

The food manufacturing industry creates opportunities in **engineering, food science, research and development, production, technology, and innovation**. However, students and graduates may know the products without knowing about the specialist careers behind them.

FutureBite aims to close that awareness gap. It combines a welcoming careers website with a distinctive outreach idea: **QR codes on everyday food packaging** that introduce people to relevant career pathways.

🏆 **Recognition:** Developed by a winning team at the BCU × Mondelēz hackathon and invited to showcase the concept at the **BCU STEAM Summit on 20 November 2026**.

> **The big question:** What if scanning your favourite snack could help you discover your future career?

## 💡 The problem we wanted to solve

- **Low awareness:** Graduates may be unfamiliar with the breadth of technical and specialist roles in food manufacturing.
- **Fragmented discovery:** Generic recruitment platforms do not always explain industry-specific career pathways clearly.
- **Uncertainty:** Early-career candidates need relatable descriptions of roles, required skills, working environments and next steps.
- **Employer reach:** Food manufacturers need ways to engage promising talent, including people who are not already searching for food industry jobs.

## 🎯 Who is FutureBite for?

**Primary audiences:** university students, recent graduates and early-career professionals exploring engineering, science and technology pathways.

**Additional audiences:** researchers, postgraduate students, food scientists, engineers, R&D specialists, and industry partners interested in reaching emerging talent.

## 📣 Beyond the QR code: reaching the right talent

Our hackathon proposal combines several complementary discovery channels:

- **Product packaging:** turn a familiar consumer interaction into an invitation to discover careers.
- **University engagement:** work with careers teams, academic departments and alumni communities.
- **Digital outreach:** use LinkedIn, Instagram and search visibility to reach relevant audiences.
- **Industry networks:** collaborate with food-science, engineering and professional communities to build trust and awareness.

A QR code alone will not identify qualified candidates. It is an **awareness channel** within a broader, targeted strategy.

## 🚀 Where the idea could go next

The following are **proposed next steps**, not claims about features already deployed:

1. **Pilot QR-enabled packaging** with manufacturer approval, using a limited product range or campaign.
2. **Measure engagement** through QR scans, site visits, quiz completions and relevant career-page interactions.
3. **Improve career matching** with richer role descriptions and more tailored suggestions.
4. **Connect to verified vacancies** and clear application journeys, where employer integrations are available.
5. **Test with students and graduates** to improve usability, accessibility and content relevance.
6. **Review privacy, analytics and packaging requirements** before any commercial rollout.

**Success would mean more than scans:** the meaningful outcome is helping suitable candidates discover, understand and pursue opportunities they might otherwise overlook.
